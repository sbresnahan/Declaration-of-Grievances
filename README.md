# A Declaration of Grievances

> “To prove this, let Facts be submitted to a candid world.”
>
> — *The Declaration of Independence*, 1776

**A Declaration of Grievances** is an independent, source-linked editorial record of the second administration of Donald J. Trump.

The American Declaration of Independence did more than announce a separation: it set out a series of grievances and invited the world to judge the facts behind them. This project borrows that form and its recurring *“He has…”* construction to examine the conduct of a modern presidency.

Its subjects include the use of public office for private benefit, political pressure on the justice system, threats to the press, restrictions on voting, immigration enforcement and due process, the treatment of international allies, and decisions affecting science, public health, and humanitarian assistance.

The purpose is to make a sustained argument **that readers can test against the record**.

## Read the record

As of October 22, 2026, the collection contains **27 grievances and 108 linked sources**. Each article includes:

- A concise statement of the grievance, written in the Declaration’s style.
- A summary distinguishing documented events from editorial interpretation.
- A dated sequence of significant actions, reporting, and court decisions.
- Clickable headlines leading to reporting, official documents, and other relevant records.
- An explicit statement of what the available evidence does *not* establish.

The homepage is deliberately spare. The supporting record sits one click beneath each grievance, where it can be examined independently of the rhetoric.

## Editorial principles

This is an **editorial project, not a legal indictment or an official historical document**. Its judgments are stated openly; they are not presented as findings of a court.

The collection follows several principles:

1. **Specificity.** Describe identifiable actions and decisions rather than relying on broad characterizations.
2. **Traceability.** Link readers to attributable journalism, legal records, and primary materials whenever available.
3. **Context.** Include consequential responses from the administration and distinguish proposals from enacted policy.
4. **Uncertainty.** Identify contested allegations, disputed responsibility, preliminary rulings, and gaps in the evidence.
5. **Correction.** Revise claims when credible new evidence or later legal outcomes require it.

The inclusion of a source does not imply that every allegation in it has been independently proven. Reporting is a record of what was known at publication, and court proceedings may continue after an article appears. External material remains the work of its respective publishers and authors.

## A continuing record

The collection is selective rather than exhaustive and will evolve as events unfold. Well-supported corrections and additions are welcome through GitHub Issues. A useful submission identifies the relevant grievance, the claim at issue, and a verifiable source for the proposed change.

The central invitation remains the same as in 1776: **examine the grievances, inspect the evidence, and judge for yourself.**
